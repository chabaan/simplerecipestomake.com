<p>The first time I cooked a hobo packet, I made the classic mistake: I used regular foil, wrapped it too loose, and ended up with burnt edges and raw potatoes. That was the last time. Now I double-layer heavy-duty foil, cut vegetables to a uniform thickness, and keep a tight seal. The result is a complete dinner—juicy ground beef, tender potatoes, sweet carrots, and soft onions—all steaming together in one neat package. No plates, no skillet to scrub, just dinner lifted straight from the coals.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/20689cf7-3ac2-4dbb-abdc-fac89e8b1997/0_0.png" alt="Open foil packet showing cooked ground beef patty and tender vegetables" /></figure>


<h2>Why This Method Works</h2>
<p>The key is even heat and even sizing. Vegetables cut to 1/4-inch rounds cook at the same rate as a 1/2-inch beef patty. The foil packet traps steam, which gently cooks everything without drying out. The double-layer prevents tearing and leaking, which means no lost juices and no flare-ups. I keep the ratio at 4 ounces of beef to about a cup of vegetables per person—enough for a satisfying main, not so much that the packet bulges and cooks unevenly.</p>

<h2>Choosing Your Foil and Building the Packet</h2>
<p>Standard foil is too thin for campfire heat. It tears, it burns, and your dinner ends up in the ashes. Use heavy-duty foil, and always double-layer it. For each packet, tear off two 18-inch sheets and stack them. This gives you a strong barrier that can handle direct contact with hot coals.</p>

<h3>Assembling the Packet</h3>
<p>Start with the vegetables. Toss them in oil and seasonings first—this coats every surface and prevents sticking. Pile them in the center of the foil. Place the beef patty on top. The patty should be flat, not a thick ball; a half-inch thickness ensures it cooks through in the same time as the vegetables. Fold the long sides together over the food, crimp twice tightly, then fold the short ends like a gift box. Leave a little air space—about an inch of room above the food—for steam to circulate. A flat, tight packet cooks faster and more evenly than a loose, puffy one.</p>

<h2>Fire Management: Coals, Not Flames</h2>
<p>Direct flames will burn the foil and char the food before the inside is done. You want a bed of hot, glowing coals with no active flames. For a campfire, let the wood burn down to embers first—about 30 minutes. For a charcoal grill, light the coals and wait until they are covered with gray ash. Spread the coals into an even layer. Place the packets directly on the coals, not on a grate above them. The coals should feel hot when you hold your hand 6 inches above them for 2 seconds—that’s medium-high heat.</p>

<p>Cook for 20 minutes, then flip with tongs and cook another 5 minutes. The exact time depends on coal temperature and packet size. After 25 minutes, remove one packet and open it carefully—steam will rush out. Check the beef with a thermometer: 160°F is safe. Vegetables should pierce easily with a fork. If not, reseal and cook 5 more minutes.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them</h2>
<p><strong>Cutting vegetables too large.</strong> Thick chunks of potato will stay hard while the beef overcooks. Stick to 1/4-inch rounds or small dice.<br>
<strong>Skipping the oil.</strong> Without oil, vegetables stick to the foil and dry out. A light coating of olive oil keeps them moist and helps seasonings adhere.<br>
<strong>Overfilling the packet.</strong> More than 1.5 cups of food per packet creates a thick bundle that cooks unevenly. Stick to one serving per packet.<br>
<strong>Opening too soon.</strong> Let the packet rest for 2 minutes off the heat before opening. This allows the steam to finish cooking the center and the juices to settle.</p>

<h2>Adapting for Camp Stove or Grill</h2>
<p>If you don’t have a campfire, a gas or charcoal grill works perfectly. Set the grill to medium-high (about 400°F) and place packets on the grate. Cook for 20 minutes, flip, and cook 5 more minutes. For a camp stove, use a cast iron skillet. Place the packets in the skillet over medium heat, cover with a lid, and cook for 20 minutes, flipping once. The skillet protects the foil from direct burner heat and prevents scorching.</p>

<h2>Make-Ahead and Storage Tips</h2>
<p>Assemble the packets at home the night before. Stack them in a large zip-top bag and keep them in the cooler with plenty of ice. Cook within 24 hours. Once cooked, any leftover packets should be eaten within 2 hours (1 hour if the air temperature is above 90°F). Reheat by placing the sealed packet back on warm coals or a grill for 5-7 minutes.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>An easy hobo packet ground beef dinner is about precision, not guesswork. Uniform cuts, a tight double-layer seal, and a bed of hot coals deliver a meal that’s cooked through, juicy, and full of flavor. Once you get the technique down, you can swap in any vegetables or protein. But start with this classic combination—beef, potatoes, carrots, and onions—and you’ll have a reliable campfire dinner that works every time.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use ground turkey instead of ground beef?</h3>
<p>Yes, ground turkey works well. Use 93/7 for less grease, and add a tablespoon of olive oil to the vegetables to prevent dryness. Cook to an internal temperature of 165°F.</p>
<h3>How do I know when the hobo packet is done?</h3>
<p>The beef should reach 160°F (165°F for poultry) on an instant-read thermometer. The vegetables should be easily pierced with a fork. At 20-25 minutes over hot coals, they should be tender. If not, reseal and cook 5 more minutes.</p>
<h3>Can I add other vegetables?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Bell peppers, zucchini, corn kernels, or green beans all work. Cut them to a similar 1/4-inch thickness so they cook evenly. Avoid watery vegetables like mushrooms unless you drain them first.</p>
<h3>What if I don't have a campfire?</h3>
<p>You can cook the packets on a gas or charcoal grill over medium-high heat (about 400°F), or in a 400°F oven on a baking sheet. The timing is the same. For a camp stove, use a cast iron skillet and cook over medium heat, flipping once.</p>
