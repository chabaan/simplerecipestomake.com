<p>I love the smell of a campfire mixing with toasted tortillas. These peanut butter wraps are my go-to for a no-fuss lunch that doesn't need a cooler. The trick is a warm, slightly crisp exterior and a cool, creamy interior—no refrigeration required.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/a1d9d791-4492-47c5-8eb1-1ab4ccfe1030/0_0.png" alt="Step-by-step assembly of a peanut butter wrap on a cutting board, with tortilla spread with peanut butter and topped with banana slices before rolling." /></figure>
<p>Here's the method: assemble at home, wrap in foil, and heat over coals at camp. The peanut butter stays stable, the banana adds sweetness, and the honey brings a touch of stickiness that caramelizes against the tortilla. No fridge, no worry.</p><h2>Why No Refrigeration Works</h2><p>Peanut butter is shelf-stable—it doesn't need cold. Bananas are fine at room temperature for a day, especially when whole. The key is the ratio: keep the filling dry enough that the tortilla doesn't get soggy. I use exactly 2 tablespoons of peanut butter per wrap, spread edge to edge. That layer seals the tortilla from moisture. Bananas go on top, not mixed in. Honey is thin, so just a drizzle—1/2 tablespoon per wrap. Too much liquid and the wrap turns to mush.</p><p>For food safety: if the temperature is above 90°F, keep wraps shaded in your pack. Below that, they're fine for 6 hours. I've kept them in a dry pack for a full morning hike and they were perfect at lunch.</p><h2>Assembly Method</h2><p>Start with a clean surface. Lay a tortilla flat. Use the back of a spoon to spread peanut butter evenly, leaving a 1/2-inch border. This border helps the wrap seal when rolled. Arrange banana slices in a single layer—no overlapping. Overlap traps moisture and creates a wet spot. Drizzle honey, then a pinch of cinnamon if you like warmth.</p><p>Now the roll: fold the left and right edges inward about an inch. Then roll from the bottom, snug but not tight. You want the tortilla to hold without tearing. Press the seam gently to seal. Place seam-side down on a 12-inch square of heavy-duty foil. Fold the foil over, crimp the edges into a tight packet. Double-wrap if you're worried about ash or if the fire is hot.</p><p>I make four wraps at a time, stack them in a ziplock bag, and they travel flat in my pack.</p><h2>Campfire Heating Technique</h2><p>Let your fire burn down to glowing coals—no flames. Flames char the foil and burn the tortilla. Place the foil packets directly on the coals. Use tongs to flip after 2–3 minutes. You'll hear a faint sizzle. The wrap is ready when the tortilla feels warm and firm through the foil. Overcooking makes the banana mushy and the tortilla brittle.</p><p>If using a camp stove, set it to medium-low. Place packets on the grate, cook 2–3 minutes per side. Same visual cue: the foil will feel hot to the touch (use tongs). Let rest 1 minute before unwrapping—steam inside is hot.</p><p>Common mistake: cooking too long. The goal is warmth, not a deep toast. A light golden color on the tortilla is perfect.</p><h2>Variations for Dietary Needs</h2><p>Gluten-free? Use corn or rice tortillas. They're thinner, so reduce cooking time to 1–2 minutes per side. Vegan? Maple syrup replaces honey. Nut-free? Sunflower seed butter works, but it's thinner—add a tablespoon of chia seeds to thicken. Keto? Use almond butter and a low-carb tortilla, skip the banana (try unsweetened coconut flakes instead).</p><p>For any diet, the principle stays: dry filling, sealed wrap, gentle heat.</p><h2>Packing and Serving Tips</h2><p>Assemble at home, wrap in foil, and pack in a dry bag. No ice needed. At camp, heat and eat. If you prefer a cold wrap, skip the fire—the peanut butter and banana are satisfying at room temperature. For a crunch, add a handful of crushed peanuts or granola before rolling.</p><p>Cleanup is simple: fold the foil into a small square and pack it out. No dishes.</p><p>Takeaway: These wraps are a reliable, no-refrigeration lunch that comes together in minutes. The technique is straightforward—balance moisture, seal the packet, heat gently. You'll have a warm, satisfying meal at camp without a cooler.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I make these wraps vegan?</h3>
<p>Yes. Use maple syrup instead of honey, and choose a vegan tortilla (most flour tortillas are vegan, but check labels). Peanut butter is naturally vegan.</p>
<h3>How long do these wraps last without refrigeration?</h3>
<p>Assembled and foil-wrapped, they stay safe at room temperature for up to 6 hours (below 90°F). For longer trips, pack ingredients separately and assemble at camp.</p>
<h3>Can I cook these on a camp stove instead of a fire?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Place the foil packets directly on a camp stove grate over medium-low heat. Cook 2–3 minutes per side, just until the tortilla is warm and slightly crisp.</p>
<h3>What if I don't have heavy-duty foil?</h3>
<p>Use two layers of regular foil, or a foil pan with a lid. The key is a sealed packet that traps steam and heat.</p>
