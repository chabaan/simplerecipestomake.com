<p>The smell of butter sizzling in a hot cast iron skillet over an open fire. The sight of dough puffing and browning in golden spots. That first bite of a warm, cinnamon-sugar-coated donut ball, crisp on the outside and fluffy inside. This is campfire donuts with just five ingredients, no deep fryer, and no fuss.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/633ac78a-fdca-4067-ae65-c44de04d4bd5/0_2.png" alt="Raw biscuit dough pieces being cut into quarters on a cutting board at a campsite." /></figure>


<h2>Why This Method Works</h2>
<p>Most campfire donut recipes call for deep frying in oil. That means carrying a bottle of oil, dealing with hot splatters, and disposing of used oil at the campsite. I skip the fryer entirely by using butter in a cast iron skillet. The butter adds richness and helps the dough brown evenly. The covered cooking steams the inside while the bottom crisps, then a quick uncovered finish gives you a golden crust all around.</p>
<p>The ratio matters: one can of biscuits to one-quarter cup of butter. That's enough fat to coat the pan and let the dough absorb just a little, keeping the donuts light. Too much butter and they get greasy. Too little and they stick.</p>

<h2>Fire Management for Even Heat</h2>
<p>Campfire cooking is all about coal management. For these donuts, you want a medium heat — about 350°F. How to check without a thermometer? Hold your hand about 6 inches above the cooking grate. If you can keep it there for 4-5 seconds before it feels too hot, you're in the right zone.</p>
<p>Build your fire off to one side of the fire ring. Let it burn down to a bed of coals, then rake a small pile of coals under the grate. You want a single layer of hot coals, not a towering flame. If the fire flares up, move the skillet to the side or lift the grate slightly. Safety first: always keep a bucket of water or sand nearby.</p>

<h3>Skillet Placement</h3>
<p>Place the skillet directly on the grate. If you're using a tripod, adjust the chain so the skillet sits about 4-5 inches above the coals. I prefer a grate because it gives you a stable surface and lets you slide the skillet to a cooler spot if needed.</p>

<h2>The Simple Ingredient List</h2>
<ul>
<li>1 can (16.3 oz) refrigerated buttermilk biscuits — the flaky layers work perfectly here</li>
<li>1/4 cup unsalted butter — salted butter works too, just reduce any added salt</li>
<li>1/3 cup granulated sugar</li>
<li>1 teaspoon ground cinnamon</li>
<li>Optional: 1/4 teaspoon vanilla extract stirred into the melted butter (this is my secret for extra flavor, but it's optional so it doesn't count as a sixth ingredient)</li>
</ul>
<p>The cinnamon-sugar ratio I use is 1 teaspoon cinnamon to 1/3 cup sugar. That gives a warm spice kick without overwhelming the sweetness. If you like a stronger cinnamon flavor, bump it to 1.5 teaspoons.</p>

<h2>Step-by-Step: From Dough to Donut</h2>
<p><strong>1. Prep the dough.</strong> Open the can of biscuits. Separate each biscuit and cut it into four equal pieces. Roll each piece into a rough ball between your palms. This takes about 2 minutes and gives you 32 small dough balls. They don't need to be perfect spheres — rustic shapes add character.</p>
<p><strong>2. Melt the butter.</strong> Place the skillet on the grate over the coals. Add the butter and let it melt, tilting the skillet to coat the bottom evenly. The butter will foam and then settle. That's your cue to add the dough.</p>
<p><strong>3. Add the dough.</strong> Carefully drop the dough balls into the hot butter. Arrange them in a single layer — they'll spread a little as they cook. Cover the skillet loosely with a sheet of heavy-duty foil. This traps steam and cooks the centers through.</p>
<p><strong>4. Cook covered.</strong> Let them cook for 6-8 minutes. You'll see the dough puff up and the bottoms turn golden. Don't lift the foil too often; let the steam do its work.</p>
<p><strong>5. Flip and finish.</strong> Remove the foil. Using tongs and a heat-resistant spatula, flip each donut ball. The cooked side should be a deep golden brown with darker spots from the butter. Cook uncovered for another 5-7 minutes, turning occasionally, until all sides are evenly browned. The total cook time is about 12-15 minutes.</p>
<p><strong>6. Coat in cinnamon sugar.</strong> Remove the skillet from the fire. Let the donuts cool for 1 minute, then toss them in the cinnamon-sugar mixture while they're still warm. The sugar sticks to the buttery surface. Serve immediately.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them</h2>
<p><strong>Burnt bottoms.</strong> If your coals are too hot, the bottoms will blacken before the centers cook. Solution: lift the skillet a few inches above the coals using a small rock or a grate riser. Or push some coals away to reduce heat directly under the pan.</p>
<p><strong>Raw centers.</strong> If the outside is brown but the inside is doughy, you cooked at too high a heat. Next time, use fewer coals or cover the skillet for the full 8 minutes before flipping.</p>
<p><strong>Soggy donuts.</strong> This happens if you overcrowd the skillet. Cook in batches if needed. The dough balls need space to steam and crisp.</p>

<h2>Make-Ahead and Camp Tips</h2>
<p>You can pre-mix the cinnamon sugar at home and store it in a small zip-top bag. Pack the butter in a small container. Keep the biscuit can in your cooler — it doesn't need to be ice-cold, just cool. If the dough gets too warm, it becomes sticky and harder to handle. Pop it back in the cooler for 10 minutes before cutting.</p>
<p>For a variation, add a pinch of nutmeg or cardamom to the cinnamon sugar. Or skip the cinnamon and coat the donuts in powdered sugar mixed with a little cocoa powder for a chocolate version.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>Five ingredients, one skillet, and a bed of coals. That's all it takes to pull off warm, cinnamon-sugar donuts at the campsite. The key is managing your fire temperature and giving the dough enough time to cook through under cover. Once you nail that, you'll be making these on every trip.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use homemade biscuit dough instead of canned?</h3>
<p>Yes, you can use your favorite homemade biscuit dough. Just make sure it's slightly sweet (add a tablespoon of sugar to the dry ingredients) and cut into small pieces. The cook time may vary by a minute or two depending on thickness.</p>
<h3>How do I know when the donuts are fully cooked?</h3>
<p>The donuts should be deep golden brown on all sides. Break one open to check: the center should be fully set, not doughy or sticky. If dough clings to your finger, return them to the skillet for another 2-3 minutes.</p>
<h3>Can I make these on a camp stove instead of a campfire?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Use a camp stove on medium heat (about 350°F). Follow the same steps but cover the skillet with a lid or foil. The cooking time will be similar, but check the bottom to prevent burning.</p>
<h3>What if I don't have a cast iron skillet?</h3>
<p>You can use a heavy-duty aluminum foil pan placed on a grate over coals, or a Dutch oven. If using a foil pan, place it on a stable surface and stir the donuts gently to avoid tearing the foil.</p>
